>Keypad emulation
If you look in the code in TOSHELL.OPL you'll see something like
    name$="sys$shll.*"
    pid%=call($0188,addr(name$)+1)
and if you look for "word.*" instead you'll be able to jump to any
running instance of the Word Processor (see the display in Spy for
the "process names" used by the various different apps).

>Modifying TOSHELL.OPL (a few more hints)
Sorry, the TOSHELL.OPL on my hard disk was a bit different from the
one in the FLIST.  The lines
  ax%=$0100
  os($88,...)
are roughly equivalent to the CALL($0188,...) I mentioned.
Yes you're right that the 26 is the character keycode.  In $404, the
$4 in the top byte is the "mask" of modifiers you're interested in
(here, you're interested in the Control modifier), and the $04 in the 
bottom byte is the value you require for the modifiers you've
declared an interest in (ie either set - as here - or unset).  Thus
with a CX% parameter of $404, you definitely want to see the Control
modifier set, but you don't care about any other modifier either way.
And then 26 just happens to be the standard ASCI value for Control-Z. 
But watch out if you try to capture eg Control-I, since you'll
capture Control-Tab at the same time, and likewise Control-M is
indistinguishable from Control-Enter.
Finally, you shouldn't do your machine any damage with the os-calls
in TOSHELL (so long as you don't change their numbers, ie change them 
into other os-calls).
Regards, DavidW
